## Introduction: The Mid Game Transition

What the hell is Deadlock's mid game? If you've played Deadlock for even a couple of hours, that moment has come to you. You're in lane phase, things are making sense. You trade kills, you push creeps, and you try and get that tower at the end. Then, suddenly, some towers start to fall, objectives appear all around the map, and the team scatters out of your lane.

Deadlock is sneaky. It's a hero shooter with some extra rules. If you come from others, like Overwatch 2, Rivals, or hell, even Apex, you can survive lane phase along pure skill and game sense alone. But once towers fall, Deadlock reveals its true identity: **It's a MOBA wearing a hero shooter's jacket.** And for the rest of the game, you'll be playing on these brand new terms.

## Wave Management and Macro: The Unglamorous Truth

Here's the thing: **ignoring wave is the fastest way to lose the game.** As people chase kills in the jungle and fights over urn, walkers fall while nobody's looking. And regardless of rank, bad macro is bad gameplay. It isn't the most glamorous job, but somebody's gotta do it.

### What is Macro?

If you're newer to MOBAs, this term "macro" might be confusing. Let's break it down: **It's everything beyond the immediate duel.** It's how our objectives are standing, how our lanes are pushed, and how our rotations are done on time to ensure that those walkers don't take some extra creep damage.

> **Transcript Quote:** "It's everything beyond the immediate duel. It's how our objectives are standing, how our lives are pushed, and how our rotations are done on time to ensure that those walkers don't take some extra creep damage."

Macro is also **communicating when you can't cover lane.** If you see creeps crashing your side lane walker while you're on the other side in the middle of the jungle, call it out. The lower your rank, the more likely it is that your teammates are suffering from tunnel vision and ignoring the vital objective.

**This is the first truth of Deadlock's mid game.**

We've been talking about objectives and walkers, but what the hell are they? Here are some walker stats to help you know exactly why they're so important:

- **Side lane walkers:** [VERIFY: 5,800 HP]
- **Blue lane walker:** [VERIFY: Over 9,000 HP]
- **Aura effect:** [VERIFY: 15% Spirit and Bullet Resist] to allies nearby

### Why Do Walkers Matter?

**Because they change the economy of the entire game.**

- One walker down = one extra slot
- Three walkers down = two extra slots

This is both an economic and power advantage over the opposing team and can ensure that the next fight you take is one where your team is heavily bolstered in comparison.

**Remember: wasting time poking walkers pre-minute [VERIFY: 16] is bait.** The walker resistances are too high at this point, and your team starts to bleed tempo. The real move is to shove the wave, steal their jungle, and wait for that more opportune time to strike.

## The 5-Minute Combat Cycle: Deadlock's Metronome

Every [VERIFY: 5 minutes] in Deadlock, urn and bridge buffs respawn. Think of these like timers in your head, the metronome of mid game. Teams naturally gravitate to these spawns, more so bridge buffs than urn. However, they are the catalyst for many a team fight.

### It's Not About Taking Objectives—It's About Timing

The key in these objective and win mechanics is not simply just taking them when they spawn, but rather **taking them at the most opportune time.** If you treat every 5-minute mark as a checkpoint in the game, it more easily organizes your play.

> **Transcript Quote:** "The key in these objective and win mechanics is not simply just taking them when they spawn, but rather taking them at the most opportune time. If you treat every 5 minute mark as a checkpoint in the game, it more easily organizes your play."

### The Optimal Cycle

Between minutes [VERIFY: 10 and 14:30], you should really be farming and trying to get your next power spike. Then, as soon as that bridge buff comes online or that urn finally drops with your newly acquired item, you can take that fight.

**The worst thing you can do at this point is still scrambling for camps and boxes and sitting on over [VERIFY: 2,000] in souls.** This is where so many teams lose: they show up late to the fight without their power spike and are surprised when they get rolled.

> **Transcript Quote:** "And this is where so many teams lose, they show up late to the fight without their power spike and are surprised when they get rolled. The urn doesn't win you to fight, the preparation does. The urn is merely the catalyst to get it going."

## Power Spikes and Power Ramping

Let's dig into that preparation and learn some new terms: **power ramping** and **power spikes.**

### What is a Power Spike?

A power spike is when your character goes from "eh" to "oh god, kill it now." It can be an item like Tesla Bullets for Wraith or Ivy, or maybe even an upgraded ability like tier 3 on Kelvin's Ice Beam.

The key is there are points in the game where you yourself are markedly stronger than you were before. And in relation to the rest of the lobby, you probably have a higher total power cap than others at that exact moment.

> **Transcript Quote:** "A power spike is when your character goes from eh to oh god, kill it now. It can be an item like Tesla bullets for Wraith or Ivy, or maybe even an upgraded ability like tier 3 on Kelvin's Ice Beam."

### Spikes Don't Last Forever

However, these spikes don't last forever. Using Tesla Bullets or hitting that tier 3 is very nice and can get you through that next fight. However, relying on it for more than [VERIFY: 2 or 3 minutes] can seriously start to see some diminishing returns.

As soon as the enemy hits their power spikes, then the tables are yet again even if not flipped back on you. **Those couple minutes in between are your window, and are exactly when you should be taking either those urn fights or pushing an objective.** If you simply wait on it, your spike is wasted.

> **Transcript Quote:** "However, relying on it for more than 2 or 3 minutes can seriously start to see some diminishing returns. As soon as the enemy hits their power spikes, then the tables are yet again even if not flipped back on you. Those couple minutes in between are your window, and are exactly when you should be taking either those urn fights or pushing an objective."

### Syncing Spikes with the Combat Cycle

Power spikes with that 5-minute combat cycle is so essential. If you and 2 or 3 other of your teammates all have just bought and are ready for a fight along with the urn dropping, then you and your team are cooking up something sweet.

## Farm Priority: Hitting Your Spikes on Time

With all this talk of power spikes and power ramping, it begs the question: how do I hit my spikes on time? **Farming.**

### Farm Priority Hierarchy

Here is your farm priority list:

1. **Center sacrifices** - Should be focused above all, primarily due to the buffs they can give you at a jackpot hit. This will give you [VERIFY: 4 boons] that you typically get from a golden statue and is a fantastic way to continually ramp your passive stats.

2. **Jungle creeps** - After center sacrifices, we move on to jungle creeps.

> **Transcript Quote:** "Center sacrifices should be focused above all, primarily due to the buffs they can give you at a jackpot hit. This will give you 4 boons that you typically get from a golden statue and is a fantastic way to continually ramp your passive stats."

Think of this as your farm hierarchy. When you're pushing the wave and get it up to walker, instead of poking that walker at those early minutes, rotate into their jungle and start to steal their resources.

## Soul Denial: Macro-Level Economy Control

This is **macro soul denial.** In lane phase, you can deny by shooting the little soul that comes out of the creep. In mid game, you can deny by choking out entire soul camps.

This again creates a widened differential between their total possible soul allocation as well as your total possible soul allocation. If your team farms efficiently and invades smartly, this leads to quicker buys and better buys for that next urn, bridge buff, or mid boss fight.

> **Transcript Quote:** "This is macro soul denial. In lane phase, you can deny by shooting the little solar that comes out of the creep. In mid game, you can deny by choking out entire soul camps. This again creates a widened differential between their total possible soul allocation as well as your total possible soul allocation."

### The Snowball Effect

And the beauty of this denial is that **it snowballs.** The more you and your team are able to smartly steal, the larger the differential gets, the harder it will be for them to push you out of their own jungle, yet again allowing you to soak up even more of their farm.

The more they're starved, the fewer options they have, and the more likely it is for you to not only steal those resources, but then rotate back into lane and finally hit that walker for some damage.

**So whenever you're not fighting or rotating, ask yourself: what's the highest value farm I could be taking right now?** If the answer is boxes in your own jungle, well, you're probably leaving a whole lot of soul on the table.

> **Transcript Quote:** "So whenever you're not fighting or rotating, ask yourself, what's the highest value farm I could be taking right now? If the answer is boxes in your own jungle, well, you're probably leaving a whole lot of soul on the table."

## Map Awareness: Free Information

Because the map is free info. You can see where enemies are, primarily **where they aren't,** and if a wave is pushing too far into one of your lanes. And from all this info, you start to predict and intuit what the next move might be.

### Example: Reading the Map

As an example, let's say you're in the far left lane and you see three or even four enemies in the mid and right side of the map. You can safely say that this could max mean only two are in your lane. You could do two things:

1. **Rotate and try to help your team** - However, since you're on the other side of the map, this could take quite a while.

2. **Shove the lane** (BETTER OPTION) - The much more advantageous option would be to try and shove this lane due to the heavy allocation of enemy team members on the other side of the map. It should be much easier for you to try and do damage on that walker or at the very least invade and steal some farm.

> **Transcript Quote:** "As an example, let's say you're in the far left lane and you see three or even four enemies in the mid and right side of the map. You can safely say that this could max mean only two are in your lane... The much more advantageous option would be to try and shove this lane due to the heavy allocation of enemy team members on the other side of the map."

### The Danger State: No Enemies Visible

Conversely, **if you see no one on the enemy team, this is a much more dangerous state to be in.** Pushing the lane is more than likely going to result in you getting ganked. This would result in not only you, but possibly a teammate trying to come and save you dying, leading to less souls per minute and more than likely either walker damage on your side or at the very least a jungle invasion.

## Building Intuition: Pattern Recognition

This is where your intuition needs to start coming into play. **Intuition is simply practiced pattern recognition.** And the only way to build up that, well, play more games.

The more you watch the map and the more you start playing, the more you'll be able to predict what the enemy team is likely concocting:

- What's their win condition right now?
- Where do they need to be in order to get the best advantage?
- What are possibly their next buys?

> **Transcript Quote:** "Intuition is simply practiced pattern recognition. And the only way to build up that, well, play more games. The more you watch the map and the more you start playing, the more you'll be able to predict what the enemy team is likely concocting."

Take all of this info and let it affect your gameplay, trying to **counter buy, counter rotate, and even counter push.** That's the thought process that turns raw awareness into anticipation and perfect execution.

And the best teams do this collectively, constantly communicating and coordinating what they feel might be happening. **Deadlock rewards the predictors, not the reactors.**

> **Transcript Quote:** "Deadlock rewards the predictors, not the reactors."

## Bringing It All Together: The Six Principles

Mid game is the pivot point of the match. It's when the facade of the hero shooter finally falls and the MOBA comes out. No longer is the game only worried about your lane, but rather all three at the same time.

To play it well, you need to adhere to these principles:

1. **Respect lane pressure** - Wave management is not optional
2. **Protect and punish walkers** - They change the entire economy
3. **Align with the 5-minute combat cycle** - Use it as your metronome
4. **Hit your power spikes on time** - Farm efficiently to reach them
5. **Farm and deny efficiently** - Maximize your souls, minimize theirs
6. **Train your map sense until it becomes second nature** - Prediction > Reaction

> **Transcript Quote:** "To play it well, you need to adhere to those principles. Respect lane pressure, protect and punish walkers, align with that five minute combat cycle, hit your power spikes on time, farm and deny efficiently, and above all, train your map sense until it becomes second nature."

So the next time you hear those towers fall and you ask yourself, "What the hell is Deadlock's mid game?" **You'll have your answer.**
